Kebbeh bil Sanieh (Lebanese Baked Kebbeh Tray)

Prep + Cook Time	2 hours 
Servings	16
Tags	Lebanese, Levantine, lamb, make-ahead, freezer-friendly, crowd-feeding, one-tray

Ingredients:
For the shell

 1000g fine bulgur (#1 grade)
 1400g lean ground lamb, from leg or shoulder
 2 medium onion, quartered
 3 tsp ground allspice
 2 tsp ground cinnamon
 1 tsp black pepper
 4 tsp salt
 240ml ice water
For the filling

 800g ground lamb, roughly 20% fat
 4 medium onions, finely diced
 160g pine nuts
 6 tbsp ghee or clarified butter
 2 tsp ground allspice
 1 tsp ground cinnamon
 2 tsp salt
 4 tbsp pomegranate molasses
For the tray

 160ml olive oil, for greasing and drizzling

Steps for preparation:
Rinse the fine bulgur in a sieve under cold running water, then transfer it to a bowl, cover with cold water, and leave it to soften for 15 minutes. Drain it in the sieve and then squeeze it firmly between your hands to force out the water. It should feel damp and springy, never wet.
While the bulgur soaks, melt the ghee in a wide pan over medium heat. Add the diced onions and cook for about 10 minutes, stirring now and then, until they are soft and lightly golden.
Add the fattier ground lamb to the onions, breaking it apart with the back of a spoon so it browns evenly rather than clumping. Cook until no pink remains, roughly 6 to 8 minutes.
Stir the allspice, cinnamon, salt, and pomegranate molasses into the meat. Cook for 2 more minutes so the spices bloom, then take the pan off the heat.
In a separate dry pan over low heat, toast the pine nuts, shaking the pan constantly. They go from pale to golden in under 3 minutes and burn within seconds of that, so pull them off while they are still lighter than you want. Stir them into the filling and set it aside to cool completely.
Make the shell. Put the quartered onion in a food processor and blitz it to a paste. Add the lean lamb, allspice, cinnamon, black pepper, and salt, and process until the mixture is smooth and almost pasty.
Add the squeezed bulgur to the processor in three batches, pulsing between each and adding the ice water one spoonful at a time. Stop when the mixture is a smooth, slightly tacky dough that holds its shape when you press a piece between your fingers. Do not let it turn wet.
Heat the oven to 200C. Brush a 33 x 23 cm baking tray with some of the olive oil.
Set a bowl of cold water next to the tray and wet your hands. Take half the shell mixture and press it evenly across the base of the tray in a layer about 1cm thick, re-wetting your hands whenever the dough starts to stick.
Spread the cooled filling over the base in a level layer, leaving a 1cm border clear around the edge so the two shell layers can seal together.
Take golf-ball-sized pieces of the remaining shell mixture and flatten each one between wet palms into a thin patty. Lay the patties over the filling like tiles, overlapping slightly, then smooth the seams with wet fingers until the top is one continuous unbroken sheet.
Dip a knife in water and cut diagonal lines across the tray in both directions to form diamonds, pressing all the way down to the base. Run a wet finger around the inside edge of the tray to loosen the sides.
Press your thumb into the centre of each diamond to make a shallow well, then drizzle the remaining olive oil generously over the whole surface, letting it pool into the scored lines and the wells.
Bake for 40 to 45 minutes, until the top is deep golden brown and the kebbeh has visibly pulled away from the sides of the tray. If the colour is still pale at 45 minutes, finish it under the grill for 2 to 3 minutes, watching it closely.
Rest the tray for 10 minutes before serving, then cut through the scored lines and lift the diamonds out with a spatula. Serve warm or at room temperature.
